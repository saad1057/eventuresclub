A custom tour, here, is not a blank map and a promise to make anything possible. It is a published route, or a pair of them, rewritten around your dates, your people and the way you like to spend a day. The [planner](/plan/tour) is three steps because the useful facts fit in three steps. Places. Shape. You. The column beside the form is the brief as it stands, so you are not asked to remember what you ticked.

## Step one: places

Places are a list, not a single valley, because plenty of good trips are two valleys and a driving day between them. Swat and Kumrat are the common pair. Hunza and Skardu are the long one. Ticking six names for a long weekend does not create a six-valley trip. It creates a reply that will cross most of them out and tell you why. If you arrived from a route page, the brief remembers which one. Starting from the [Hunza page](/tours/hunza-valley) is different from starting from a blank form, and we price with that spine in view.

## Step two: shape, the part that moves the price

Shape is the part that moves the money. Season, or a real date. Group size. Where the car starts, which for most of our travellers is Lahore and can be Islamabad, Karachi or somewhere else if you say so. Length. Comfort: simple and local, standard, or premium. A budget band, including the honest option of not being sure. Notes, which should carry the fact no menu can hold. A parent who cannot do hillside stairs changes the hotel. A wish to walk changes Kumrat. A refusal to be on the road after dark changes where the day ends. None of that is a special request. It is the plan.

## Step three: you

You, the last step, is a name, an email and a phone. The email is how we reply. The phone is for the day something on the road changes, not because we prefer a call to a written note. When you send the form, two messages go out with every field labelled: one to the desk, one to you. Yours is the record of what you asked. Ours is the same record, so a reply can quote it without you wondering whether a line was lost. If you are writing from a preview of the site where mail is not connected, the brief is stored instead of sent, and the page says so. On the live site, the mail goes, or you are told it did not and given the address to use directly.

## What happens after you send the brief

What we do with it is read it against the road. A five-day Hunza request in October for four people at standard comfort is close to the published PKR 85,000 starting point, and the reply will say what that includes and what the autumn hotel situation actually is. The same dates at premium comfort are a different letter. A couple instead of four is a different letter, because the vehicle does not shrink. A request that adds Khunjerab is a different length. We do not stretch the five-day page until it covers the border and then act surprised by the driving.

## A direction, not a contract

You then get a direction, not a contract. It names the nights, the driving days, the kind of room, what is included, and the price. You can change it. Adding a night in Karimabad and dropping a rushed Passu stop is a normal edit. We would rather do that on the page than on the highway. Nothing is booked, and no advance is asked, until you approve that direction. The website never takes the payment. An invoice does, by transfer, when there is something to hold.

## Two versions, shown side by side

If two versions are close, we will show both rather than hide the cheaper one. A standard room in Karimabad and a well-known cliff hotel are not the same product, and the letter should let you see the gap before you feel obliged to choose the famous name. The same is true of a jeep that is shared with another group versus one that is yours for the morning. Shared can be fine at Saif-ul-Malook in the quiet weeks. It is a poor idea in August if the point of the trip was not to queue. Say which you want. Silence is read as standard and private for the legs the route already includes, which is what the published pages describe.

## Your no is part of the route

A brief can also say no. No mall, no chairlift, no night driving, no drone, no festival unless it was arranged. Those refusals make the plan faster, not ruder. The desk is not collecting preferences so it can ignore them in favour of a template. The template is only the spine. Your no is part of the route.

## Events use a separate planner

Events do not travel through this form. A wedding, a dinner or an offsite has its own planner, with guests and catering instead of passes and jeeps. If your trip is also a gathering — a birthday in Swat, a small nikah with a night in the hills — say so in the notes or send the [event brief](/plan/event) as well. We will keep the paperwork apart and the plan joined. That is the whole method. One desk, two doors, and a copy of your own words in your inbox before anyone promises you a lake.
