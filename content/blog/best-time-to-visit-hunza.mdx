Hunza is open for much of the year. The version people mean when they say Hunza — fruit trees, a blue lake, and a road that is not a negotiation with snow — runs from April to October. Inside that window the months are not interchangeable. April is blossom and cold nights. July is green and busy. Late September and October are the apricot and poplar colour that fills every photograph, and they are the weeks that disappear first.

From Lahore the trip is not a scenic afternoon. Our [five-day Hunza route](/tours/hunza-valley) is two long roads around one full day in the valley. You sleep on the Karakoram Highway on the way up, usually Besham or Chilas, and you sleep there again on the way down. Karimabad itself gets the unhurried day: the forts, Attabad, and Passu if that is what you asked for. Khunjerab Pass, at 4,693 metres, is not hidden inside those five days. It needs another day and a body that is comfortable with the height. Asking for the border, both forts, Eagle's Nest and a leisurely Passu morning before lunch is how a good valley becomes a sequence of car windows.

April and May are for people who like the orchards and can take a cool evening. The high passes that shortcut the journey, Babusar especially, may still be shut, so the Karakoram Highway is the reliable line. Hotels are easier than they will be in October. The lake can be extraordinary and the peaks are still carrying snow. If your group includes children or older parents, these months are kinder than a July weekend when every family in the country has had the same idea.

June through August is the main season and the most crowded. Attabad has boats, the forts have queues, and the popular rooms above Karimabad are the ones that need booking rather than hoping. This is also when Babusar is usually open, which can change the shape of the drive if you want that crossing instead of the full highway both ways. Summer Hunza is not a mistake. It is a place that needs a realistic driving day and a hotel you have actually named. Our starting price, PKR 85,000 a person for four in a standard room, is this season's ordinary version. The famous sunrise hotels are a premium quote.

September is the hinge. The rush thins, the light improves, and the first colour arrives in the orchards. It is, for many people who have been more than once, the best month that is still easy to get a room. October is the month in the posters. Poplars go gold, the days are clear, and nights in Karimabad, around 2,500 metres, ask for a proper layer. If October is the reason for the trip, send dates early. A brief that arrives in September for the first week of October is often a conversation about the second week, or about a standard hotel instead of the one on the cliff.

November can still be beautiful in the valley floor, with fewer people and a sharper cold. High roads and the pass are not a plan. Winter Hunza is a different trip: possible along the highway for people who want quiet and are honest about heating, closed for anyone whose idea of the north is a jeep to a meadow. We will say which of those you are asking for, rather than selling December as a discounted October.

Deosai does not belong in the same sentence as a short Hunza week unless you have seven days or more and the plateau is open, roughly June to September. The [Hunza and Skardu](/tours/hunza-skardu) route exists for that longer line, and even then Deosai replaces a Skardu day instead of being stapled onto a fort visit. At around 4,000 metres it is a health question as well as a scenery question. Tell us about breathing or heart conditions before we put it on the page.

What to do with this, practically: choose the month for the reason you are going, not for a general sense that the north is nice in summer. If the reason is colour, protect October and accept that the hotel you want may already be gone. If the reason is a family weekend that grew into a week, take May or September and keep Khunjerab off the list. If you are unsure, the [tour planner](/plan/tour) lets you pick a season without inventing a date, and the reply will say what is actually open. The packing piece sits beside this one, because even a July night in Karimabad is not a July night in Lahore.

One more constraint from Lahore is the public holiday. Eid and the long weekends push traffic onto the same highway nights we use for the sleep stops. A Hunza week that starts on the eve of a holiday is still possible, and it should leave earlier and sleep sooner. We would rather drop a tea-stall viewpoint than put a tired driver into the dark above Chilas. If your dates are glued to a school holiday, say that in the first note. The valley will still be there. The room above it might not, and the road will be shared with everyone else who had the same week off.
