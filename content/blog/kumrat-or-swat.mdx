Swat is the green valley people already half know. Kumrat is the forest at the end of a jeep track. They sit close enough on a map to be confused, and far enough in character that choosing the wrong one makes a good week feel like the wrong trip. From Lahore, both are Khyber Pakhtunkhwa journeys. Neither is a Hunza substitute, and neither needs to be.

Swat begins in orchards and a wide river. Mingora and the lower valley are low. Kalam, near 2,000 metres, is as far as the river road usually goes. The road up is narrow and famous, and it should not be stacked with Malam Jabba and Mahodand on the same day. Our [three-day Swat and Kalam route](/tours/swat-kalam) stays in the valley: the drive in and a night there, a river day through Madyan and Bahrain, on to Kalam if the road and the group allow it, and the drive home. Pickup is Lahore or Islamabad. The rate is PKR 16,000 a person in a sharing room, or PKR 43,500 for a couple in a separate room. Mahodand and its jeep belong on a longer plan. July can be wet, and the jeep track is the first thing to close.

Kumrat asks for a rougher ending. You spend a full day getting toward Dir, then a jeep under old deodars to a simple place by the river. The forest is the point. Kala Chashma suits most groups. Jahaz Banda is a hike for people who brought the right shoes and said so. Katora Lake, high and very blue, is a reward when the day is clear and the group is fit. It is not a backdrop you can demand at 4 p.m. after a late start. The [four-day Kumrat trip](/tours/kumrat-valley) starts at PKR 52,000 a person for four, in a private vehicle with the forest jeeps included. Comfort here is honestly simple. Paying for premium mostly improves the night in Dir, not a resort that does not exist under the trees. The phone signal is patchy. Tell the people who worry before you go, not from the valley.

Crowds follow the road. Swat and Kalam in July and August are full of families, jeeps and tea stalls, which some groups love and some groups spend the drive resenting. Kumrat is quieter because the last kilometres filter people out. It is not empty, and it is not a secret. It is simply harder to arrive at by accident. If your group wants restaurants, a choice of beds and an easy evening walk, choose Swat. If your group wants dark, river noise and a day without a mall, choose Kumrat.

Doing both is the right answer when you cannot choose and you have five days. The [Swat and Kumrat route](/tours/swat-kumrat) takes the river first, crosses toward the forest, and comes home once. That matters. Approaching each valley from Lahore on separate trips wastes two long drives. Five days is the shortest version that still gives each place a night. The link between them is the long day: an early jeep to Mahodand, back in Kalam by midday, then the road toward Dir for as far as daylight allows. Nothing else gets added to it, because that is how luggage and tempers get left in Kalam. The starting price for the joined trip is PKR 62,000 a person for four, with both jeep legs included.

Season overlaps. May to early October is the shared window. Swat can start a little earlier, in April, when the lower valley is already kind and Kumrat's higher track may still be sulking. September is the generous month for both: fewer vehicles, better light, and a forest that has not yet shut for the year. Winter is not a Kumrat plan. Swat's lower towns can be visited, but that is a different, smaller trip than the one on these pages.

Who should not force the combination: anyone with a single weekend, anyone who needs to be back for a Monday flight without slack, and anyone who wants Hunza's architecture and lake in the same week. A weekend from Lahore is Nathiagali, or a sprint to Swat or Naran. Hunza is its own road. Kumrat plus Swat is already a full five days of green north. Adding a fort in Karimabad is a fantasy of the map.

If you are still split, send the [tour brief](/plan/tour) with both valleys ticked and a sentence about noise. Quiet people get Kumrat, or Kumrat with a short Swat entry. People who want water, a chairlift and a town that can produce dinner get Swat and are not apologised to. The plan we write back will pick a spine. You can still change it before anyone books a room.

A practical test, if the descriptions still feel abstract: picture the last evening. In Swat that evening can include a walk by the river, a choice of places to eat, and other travellers doing the same. In Kumrat the evening is the river, a simple meal, and an early night because the interesting hours were on foot. Neither is more authentic. They are different uses of a few days away from Lahore. Families with small children usually sleep better in Swat. Pairs who have already done the river road, and who do not mind a basic bed, usually come back talking about the forest. If your group contains both kinds of person, the five-day join is the compromise that does not pretend they wanted the same thing.
