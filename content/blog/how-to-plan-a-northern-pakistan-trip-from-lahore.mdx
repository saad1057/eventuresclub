The useful first decision is not the hotel. It is how many nights you can actually spend off the motorway. Lahore to Nathiagali is an afternoon. Lahore to Naran is a dawn-to-dusk drive. Lahore to Hunza is two driving days with a night on the Karakoram Highway in between. If the calendar you have is a weekend, the honest destinations are the Galiyat or a very tight Naran. If you have five days, Hunza becomes possible and still mostly road. If you have a week, Hunza and Skardu can share it, with one full day in each valley rather than a glimpse of both.

## Count the people first

Start by naming the people. A group of four is the shape our published prices assume, because four people fill one vehicle and split the fuel, the driver and the tolls without anyone paying for an empty seat. A couple does not get half of that vehicle cost. A family of six may need a larger van. Solo travel is possible and should be priced as a private car, not as a discounted seat on a bus we do not run. Tell us the number before you fall in love with a per-person figure on a page.

## Match the season to the road

Then name the season against the road, not against the photograph. Nathiagali works from spring through autumn and has a real winter if you want snow. Saif-ul-Malook and Babusar are a late May to September matter. Swat and Kumrat are comfortable from April or May into October, with July often wet enough to close a jeep track. Hunza's famous colour is late September and October, and those weeks book. Khunjerab and Deosai are high-summer roads. A date that ignores this is not a preference. It is a plan that will be rewritten on the second day.

## Pick one published route as a spine

Pick one published route as a spine, then change it. The [Hunza Valley](/tours/hunza-valley) page is five days from Lahore and does not pretend to include the Chinese border. The [Swat and Kalam](/tours/swat-kalam) page is three days: the drive in, the river road, and the drive home. Mahodand is a longer plan. The [Naran and Kaghan](/tours/naran-kaghan) weekend says plainly that two of the three days are driving. Reading the spine stops a custom brief from asking for six valleys in four days. Custom planning exists to change pace, hotel and dates. It is a poor tool for suspending geography.

## Budget in bands, not one number

Budget in bands, not in a single brave number. Four departures are group seats, with pickup in Lahore or Islamabad. Naran is PKR 16,500 a person in a sharing room, or PKR 43,000 for a couple in a separate room. Swat is PKR 16,000 and PKR 43,500. Azad Kashmir is PKR 17,500 and PKR 46,500. Hunza with Skardu, eight days, is PKR 39,500 a person sharing or PKR 98,500 for a couple. The other routes are still priced for four people in a private vehicle: the Galiyat from PKR 38,000, Kumrat from PKR 52,000, five-day Hunza from PKR 85,000, Chitral from PKR 95,000. Premium hotels, especially the well-known ones above Karimabad, are a different quote. They are not a surprise line on the invoice after you have packed.

## What a starting price should include

Decide what the price must include before you compare it with another operator. On Naran, Swat, Azad Kashmir, and the eight-day Hunza and Skardu trip, the rate is a seat: pickup from Lahore or Islamabad, the group vehicle, and a hotel on a sharing basis or a separate room at the couple rate. The Saif-ul-Malook jeep, a Mahodand jeep, an Attabad boat and Deosai are included only when that page says so. The other routes include a private vehicle and driver from Lahore and back, a standard room on breakfast, tolls, and the jeep legs that the route cannot avoid. They do not include every lunch, fort tickets, or a chairlift. A cheap headline that excludes the jeep is not cheaper. It is a conversation you will have in a car park at 5 a.m.

## Leave a margin for the return

Leave a margin for the return. Do not book a flight out of Lahore on the night you are due back from Hunza or Chitral. The highway can lose two hours to a truck, a checkpost or weather, and the plan should protect the connection instead of hoping. If someone in the group has to be at a desk on Monday morning, say so in the brief. We would rather shorten a viewpoint than deliver you to an argument at the airport.

## The practical order, in short

The practical order, then, is short. Count the nights you truly have. Count the people who will sit in the vehicle. Match a published route to that shape. Read what the price includes. Send a [custom tour brief](/plan/tour) with the season, the dates if they are fixed, and the thing a form usually misses: a parent who should not do stairs, a wish to walk, a refusal to drive after dark. You will get the same list by email that we receive. The written plan that follows is the one you can accept or change. Nothing is held, and nothing is charged on the website, until that plan is the plan you want.

If you are still choosing between two valleys, read the notes on [Kumrat and Swat](/blog/kumrat-or-swat) and on the [best weeks in Hunza](/blog/best-time-to-visit-hunza). The packing list is separate, and it is shorter than the shops in Lahore would like.
